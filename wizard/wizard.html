Parseltongue Capsules

Offline inspector and guide · nothing here leaves this machine
Encoding is not encryption. Parseltongue's 222 transforms — base64, ciphers, Unicode styling, zero-width concealment — change how text looks. Every one is reversible by anyone, with no key. A capsule is the part that actually makes a message unreadable.

Paste a capsule

Needs no passphrase and attempts no decryption, so this is safe to run on a capsule that is not addressed to you. Reading the message itself is a terminal command — see How it works.

Paste a contact card

A contact card carries public keys only. It proves nothing on its own — anyone can produce one claiming any name. The fingerprints are what you confirm with the person by voice or in person.

The whole flow

  1. Create an identity. Two keypairs: Ed25519 to sign (proves you sent something) and X25519 to receive (lets others seal to you). The private halves are encrypted with a passphrase you choose. 
    parseltongue-capsule init --label "Your Name"
  2. Back it up immediately. There is no recovery. Lose the passphrase and every capsule ever sealed to you is permanently unreadable. 
    parseltongue-capsule backup --out identity-backup.txt
parseltongue-capsule verify-backup --in identity-backup.txt
    The second command is the one that matters — an unverified backup is a guess.
  3. Swap contact cards. Yours is public; share it anywhere. Then confirm the fingerprints with the other person over a channel you already trust. This is the one step software cannot do for you. 
    parseltongue-capsule card --out my-card.txt
parseltongue-capsule add-contact alice alice-card.txt
  4. Seal a message. 
    parseltongue-capsule seal --to alice --message "..." --out capsule.txt
  5. Send it however you like. Email, Signal, Discord, a gist, a QR code, a USB stick. It is just text. Encoding it further adds no secrecy.
  6. They open it. The message prints at their terminal and nowhere else. 
    parseltongue-capsule open --in capsule.txt --from bob

Why sealing and opening are not on this page

Two reasons, both deliberate. A second implementation of the same cryptography means two chances to get it wrong and bugs where they disagree. And a decrypted message in a browser tab is a copy in places you did not choose. The same reasoning is why no MCP tool can open a capsule either — a plaintext returned through a model lands in the conversation transcript.

What the cryptography is

PieceJob
X25519Key agreement — derive a shared secret from someone's public key alone
HKDF-SHA256Turn that shared secret into a proper encryption key
XChaCha20-Poly1305Encrypt, and detect tampering
Ed25519Sign — prove who sealed it
Argon2idProtect the vault passphrase against offline cracking

All established primitives via the @noble libraries. No custom cryptography. Each capsule uses a fresh ephemeral X25519 keypair, so compromising a sender's long-term keys later does not decrypt past capsules.

What is visible to anyone holding a capsule

  • the sender's public signing key and fingerprint
  • the recipient's encryption fingerprint — this is how you know which key opens it
  • the timestamp the sender claims
  • any metadata the sender attached, including the subject
  • the approximate length of the message

A capsule hides content, not the fact of correspondence. If who-talks-to-whom is what you need to protect, this is the wrong tool and no setting changes that.

What it does not defend against

ThreatWhy not
A compromised machineIf the endpoint sealing or opening is owned, so is the message. Encryption cannot fix a keylogger.
Traffic analysisSender, recipient, timing and size are all visible by design.
An unverified contact cardIf you never confirmed the fingerprint out of band, you may be sealing to an impostor. The card itself proves nothing.
A stolen backup + its passphraseTogether they are your identity. Anyone holding both can read everything sealed to you and seal as you.
What you do nextPasting the decrypted text into a chat, a notes app, or a model context makes a copy the capsule cannot protect.

A verified signature proves less than it sounds like

It proves the holder of a specific private key sealed that exact capsule, and that nothing has changed since. It does not prove who that person is. Binding a key to a human happens out of band, by comparing fingerprints with them directly.

The checksum on a contact card detects typos and corruption. It is not a signature and says nothing about authorship.

There is no recovery

No reset, no escrow, no support channel. That is the intended property — a recovery path is a backdoor with better branding. It is also why a verified backup is mandatory rather than advisable.
